Privacy Policy

Last updated: 10 November 2026

SYVT is a spelling game, for children and for anyone learning to spell, on Android and on iPhone and iPad. It plays with no connection at all, and an account is optional. This page says exactly what is stored, what is sent where, where it is kept, and how to get rid of it; where the two kinds of device differ, it says so.

Playing without an account

SYVT needs no sign-in. Used that way, everything the game remembers — the players on the device, their nicknames, avatars, chosen world and language, best scores, rounds played, points, the days they played and settings — is written to the app's own storage on that device and nowhere else. We never see it, and uninstalling the app deletes it.

Like any app's data, it can be part of the device's own backup, if the device makes one: to iCloud or a computer for an iPhone or iPad, to Google for an Android device. That backup is held under your own Apple or Google account, never in a SYVT account, and we cannot reach it. On an iPhone or iPad the recordings of words the voice has fetched are kept out of it; a device restored from a backup fetches them again.

Four things can still reach the internet without an account: the spoken words, the date check and App Check, all described below, and a purchase, which Google Play or the App Store handles.

What an account stores

An account exists so that the same players can be carried to another device, and a parent makes it in the app's settings for parents. It signs in with an email address and a password, with a Google account, or, on an iPhone or iPad, with Sign in with Apple. With a password, we receive the email address, and Google Firebase Authentication keeps the password only as a salted hash, never in readable form. With Google, Firebase Authentication receives the Google account's email address, together with the name and profile-picture link Google may include with a sign-in; the app uses only the email address. With Apple, the app asks for the email address and never for the name. Apple hands over your own address or, if you choose to hide it, a private relay address at privaterelay.appleid.com that Apple forwards to you, together with the identifier Apple gives your Apple Account for our apps alone. Whichever way, Firebase Authentication also keeps the dates the account was created and last used.

Once signed in, the game keeps one document per account on our server. For each player it holds the nickname (up to eight characters, freely chosen), the avatar (a short string of part numbers), the chosen world, the chosen language, the best score in each language, the number of rounds played, the points scored, when the player was made, whether words are read aloud, and the two paid settings — how much arithmetic is mixed in, and which level a round starts at. It holds the days on which the player earned a star (a day with a round of ten points or more, which is what the star chain is counted from) and how many stars a week a parent has asked of them, with the days that number was changed. It holds the player's sticker book too: which stickers have been found and how many of each, how many the day's play has given for each level and world, where each sticker is stuck on the album pages, and whether the player has played while the account had SYVT+, which keeps every album page open after it ends. For the account as a whole it holds whether the children collect stickers and which country's German spelling counts as right, and alongside those the identifiers of players deleted on another device, the time of the last change and a version number.

What we never collect

Apart from what a Google sign-in carries, no real names: Sign in with Apple is never asked for one. No age or date of birth, no address or telephone number, no photographs or files from the device, no contacts, no location, and no advertising identifier. Nothing a child types is collected beyond the nickname, which need not be a real name. There is no chat and no messaging, and nothing a player does is visible to any other user.

Where it is kept, and how it is protected

Account data sits on Google's Firebase platform, in a project we run. The database (Cloud Firestore) is in Google's European multi-region, with the data held in Belgium and the Netherlands. The server functions — redeeming a friends-and-family code; recording, moving or withdrawing a Google Play purchase; recording or moving an App Store purchase, and hearing Apple's notices about one; and clearing up after an account is deleted — run in Zurich, Switzerland; one more function, which guards the project's spending and handles no user data, runs in Belgium. Data is encrypted in transit and at rest. The database rules let an account read and write its own document and read its own SYVT+ record, and nothing else; purchase and code records are written only by our server. Google acts as our processor here, on our instructions.

No advertising, no analytics, no tracking

SYVT carries no advertising, no analytics, no crash reporting and no third-party tracking or marketing software. It sets no cookies and reads no advertising identifier, and on an iPhone or iPad it never asks to track you, because it does not. Google Analytics is switched off in the Firebase project. How a child plays stays on the device, apart from the scores, days played and settings described above while an account is signed in, and the spoken words a device asks for, described below.

What the servers log

As with any app built on Firebase, Google records the IP address and the user agent of the requests the app makes — for sign-in security, and in the logs of our server functions, which also note the account identifier when a purchase cannot be confirmed with Google Play. Those logs are kept briefly and are not used to build a profile. With each request, the Firebase software in the app also says what kind of device it runs on and which versions of the system and of the app; Google counts these to see which versions are in use, and they are linked to no account or device. The same software can give the installed app a random installation identifier, and on Android a call to our functions can carry a Firebase messaging token. Google uses both to operate the service; they identify an installation rather than a person, and we neither read them nor send messages. This website is served by GitHub Pages, which, like any web server, logs the IP address of a visit.

App Check

So that our servers can tell the real app from a program pretending to be it, the app has itself vouched for as it starts: on Android by Google Play (Play Integrity), which vouches only for a copy that Play installed, and on an iPhone or iPad by Apple's App Attest, or by DeviceCheck on the few devices without it. The answer is exchanged for a short-lived token that Firebase checks with every request the app makes to it. The token carries no account, no player and no name, and nobody keeps it to recognise the device by; Google or Apple sees the device asking.

The spoken words

The voice that reads the falling words is a set of fixed audio files on Cloudflare R2, at audio.syvt.me — used by the app and, on the two levels it reads them, by the game on this site. Nothing is generated while you play: the word is turned into a file name on your own device, and only that file name is asked for. No text, no account and no device identifier is ever sent. Cloudflare, like any web server, sees the IP address making the request and which files it asked for — and because the word list is public, those names say which words were practised. That is the honest limit of it. The app fetches a level's words in a batch; the game on this site asks for each word as its tile appears, so its requests follow the words a round actually shows. The files are the same for everyone; nothing about them is personal to a player. Switching the voice off stops every one of these requests.

The date check

Stickers are found by playing well, and each level gives a few a day. So that winding a tablet's clock forward does not refill them, the app asks the same server, audio.syvt.me, what day it is: one request for the name of a single file, made when the app starts and when it comes back from the background, whether or not the voice is on. It sends no account, player or device identifier and nothing typed or played; the only thing read from the reply is the date the server stamps on it. Cloudflare sees the IP address of that request as it does of any other. Offline, the tablet's own clock is used.

The daily reminder

SYVT can remind the children on a device to come and play, with one notification a day at most. It is off until somebody says yes: the game asks as it starts, until somebody answers — Not now asks again at the next start, Don't show again never does — and a parent can change the time or switch it off in the settings for parents. The reminder is worked out and scheduled on the device itself, from what the device already holds — which children have not earned their star yet that day, and the line SYVT says to them. Nothing is sent anywhere to make it or to show it, and no push-notification service is involved. Its settings — whether it is on, its time, and the reminders it has handed to the device — stay on that device and are never backed up to a SYVT account. The system asks for permission to show notifications when the reminder is switched on, and not before: on Android from version 13, and on every iPhone and iPad.

The star-chain widget

On an iPhone or iPad the star chain can also sit on the Home Screen, on the Lock Screen and in StandBy. The app writes what the widget shows — each child's name and world, this week's stars and the chain — into a file in its own space on the device, and the widget reads it there. Nothing is sent anywhere for it, and none of it is backed up to a SYVT account. Names appear on the Home Screen only: the Lock Screen and StandBy, which anyone near the phone can see, show a child's first letter.

SYVT+ and purchases

SYVT+ is sold as a monthly subscription or as a single payment: on Android through Google Play, and on an iPhone or iPad through the App Store. The store takes the payment, under your own agreement with Google or with Apple, and we never see or receive payment details. The app remembers on the device whether SYVT+ is active, so a paid game still works offline.

On Android, SYVT+ belongs to the SYVT account, so buying it needs one. While an account is signed in, the app tells our server about a SYVT+ purchase, so that SYVT+ follows the account to its other devices. It sends the purchase's Google Play token and which product it is. The server checks both with Google Play and then records, against the account, the product, the token, the date it recorded them, when a subscription expires, and when Google Play last confirmed it. It also records which account holds the purchase, so that one purchase serves one account: if another SYVT account already holds it, the app shows that account's email address cut down to its first letter and its domain, and moves the purchase only if you ask it to. When Google Play reports that a purchase has ended or been refunded, the app asks our server to take SYVT+ off the account. A purchase the server already knows about is reported again at most once a day.

On an iPhone or iPad, SYVT+ needs no account. The purchase belongs to your Apple Account and the app checks it on the device; without a SYVT account, nothing about it reaches us. While an account is signed in, the app also sends our server the purchase exactly as Apple signed it, so that SYVT+ follows the account to the family's other devices, Android ones included. The server checks Apple's signature against Apple's own root certificate and then records, against the account, Apple's identifier for the purchase, the product, when it was recorded, when it ends and whether it will renew, whether it was refunded, and when Apple signed what it last said about it. One purchase serves one account here too, shown and moved as above. A purchase made while somebody is signed in also carries a code worked out from the account's identifier, which lets the server match a purchase whose report never arrived, made on another of your devices, to the account. From then on Apple tells our server itself when a subscription renews or lapses, or a purchase is refunded, and the server keeps a note of each notice for 30 days, so that none is counted twice. We hold no key to ask Apple about anyone's purchases, and never ask.

On Apple devices SYVT+ can be shared through Family Sharing. A family member's copy opens SYVT+ on their own Apple devices and goes no further: the server answers it without writing anything, and only the buyer's own purchase is ever kept with a SYVT account.

Friends-and-family codes

Redeeming a code needs an account. It sends the code, your account identifier and whether a Google Play subscription is active to our server, which records the code, the account and the moment it was used, so that a code cannot be spent twice, and stores the entitlement it grants — where it came from, the code, the date, and any expiry date — against the account. So that codes cannot be guessed, the server also counts the codes an account tries, and refuses more than ten in an hour. These codes are redeemed in the app on Android. On an iPhone or iPad, Redeem a code opens Apple's own sheet for App Store offer codes, and what one opens is an App Store purchase like any other.

Data sharing

We do not sell data, and we share none of it with third parties for their own purposes. The companies involved are Google — as the operator of Firebase, where account data is stored, of Google Play, which processes purchases and vouches for the app on Android, and of Google sign-in when you choose it — Apple — as the operator of the App Store, which processes purchases on an iPhone or iPad, of App Attest and DeviceCheck, which vouch for the app there, and of Sign in with Apple when you choose it — GitHub, which serves this website, and Cloudflare, which serves the spoken words. Firebase, GitHub and Cloudflare act as our processors under contract, which binds them to protect the data at least as well as this policy does; Google Play, Google sign-in, the App Store, App Attest, DeviceCheck and Sign in with Apple work under your own agreement with Google or with Apple.

How long we keep it

Account data is kept for as long as the account exists, and the app can end that itself: Settings (for parents) → Danger zone → Delete account deletes the sign-in and all of the account's data on our servers, straight away: the players' backup, the SYVT+ it holds, its purchase records and the codes it redeemed or tried. Two things outlast it, and neither says whose it was: a code keeps its count of uses, and an App Store purchase that Apple refunded keeps the record that it was refunded, so that the same purchase cannot be brought into a new account. With Sign in with Apple, the app also asks Apple to withdraw SYVT from your Apple Account. The players themselves stay on the device. The same danger zone wipes every player's games, points, best scores and the days their star chains are counted from, and deleting a player removes them from the device and from the account on every device signed in to it. Data held only on a device goes when the app is uninstalled. The Data Deletion Policy sets all of this out step by step.

Children

SYVT is built to be safe to hand to a child of about eight and up, and so that a child gives nothing away in order to play. No account is needed. The only thing a child enters is a nickname of up to eight characters. There is no advertising, and nothing a child does is shown to anyone else. The only notification is the daily reminder, which stays off until somebody agrees to it and which a parent can switch off. Everything that costs money or leaves the app sits behind a parents' gate, a number written out in words to be typed back in digits, and so does every link out of the app: to the store's subscription settings, to this policy, to the help page and, on an iPhone or iPad, to Apple's terms of use. If you believe a child has given us more than the above, write to us and we will delete it.

Your rights

You may ask for a copy of the data held under your account, ask us to correct it, or ask us to delete it — see the Data Deletion Policy. You may also simply play without an account, in which case there is nothing on our servers to ask about. Write to hi@SYVT.me for any of these.

Changes to this policy

If we change what SYVT collects, we will update this page and the date at the top of it.
